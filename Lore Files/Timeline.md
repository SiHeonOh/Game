# Alt History Game — Notes

---

## Setting

- Post-nuclear / chem / bio world. Most nations broken into small states or civil war.
- US nukes Germany and Japan to end WW2. The Allies then turn on each other.
- **Nuclear head start buys quantity, not an earlier date.** The US still strikes in late July 1945 (about the real date) but has **four bombs** for simultaneous strikes. The **Soviets** get theirs ~3 years early (1946 vs. the real 1949).
- **Dates:** Allied hot war mid-1946 → ~1948. Nuclear exchange 1946–47. Collapse 1947–48.
- **No nuclear winter** (too few 1940s bombs). Collapse comes from plague, famine and broken systems, not climate.

## Walkers Instead of Tanks (1916 divergence)

Two divergences: **walkers in 1916** (sets the look of the world) and **fission in 1934** (drives the plot). Politics and events stay recognizable.

- **[ALT]** Britain's **Landships Committee** (set up by Churchill, 1915) backs a **walking design** instead of the tracked tank.
- **Why walkers win in WWI:** Western Front craters, trenches, wire and mud. Walkers step over them and beat tracks in trials. Everyone copies.
- **Why tanks stay minimal:** armies build factories, crews and doctrine around walkers. Tanks are tested between the wars but dismissed as niche.
- **In WW2:** walkers handle Russian mud and Stalingrad's ruins; **struggle badly with beach landings** (a major reason D-Day fails).
- **After the collapse:** rubble, gas pooling in low ground, wrecked factories and scarce fuel make walkers even more dominant. Leftover tanks are rare relics.
- **[REAL]** The Landships Committee tested giant-wheeled machines and the "pedrail" before choosing tracks; tanks debuted Sept 1916. Early tanks often broke down or got stuck (1916), and sank in mud at Passchendaele (1917). Walking machines existed: Chebyshev's walking mechanism (1878 Paris World's Fair), the "Steam Man" patent (1868), mining walking draglines (1910s). Cavalry still served in WW2 (Poland, USSR, Germany). At Omaha Beach most amphibious tanks sank. Wood gas burners powered vehicles during WW2 fuel shortages.

## Flying Warships & Hesperium

**Nuclear-powered flying warships**, finished after WW2. The one fantasy element is **Hesperium**.

**Origin**
- **[REAL]** Fermi's team (1934) claimed two new elements, **Ausonium** (93) and **Hesperium** (94). They were actually fission fragments. Fermi's 1938 Nobel partly cited this work.
- **[ALT]** Fermi recognizes fission. His team keeps the name **Hesperium** for something they find is **much heavier than they first thought**: a **stable superheavy element on the "island of stability"** (~element 114+). Element 94 is still plutonium, as in real history.
- **Italy holds the basis.** It's hidden when the program vanishes in 1943.
- **US, Soviet, German and UK forces** all get access to the tech.
- **[REAL]** The "island of stability" was predicted in the 1960s, around element 114 and above.

**The fantasy property**
- Fed enormous electric current, Hesperium **pushes against Earth's magnetic field, producing lift**.
- **[REAL]** Based on the Meissner effect (1933): superconductors push magnetic fields away and can levitate over magnets.

**Rules**
- **Needs nuclear power.** Only reactors supply enough current. Only nuclear powers can build the ships.
- **Scarce. Production is part of the fantasy.** Real reactors can't build anything past fermium (100) by neutron bombardment, because short-lived isotopes break the chain. **Italy's records hold a secret method**, which is the real prize. Production is slow; only a handful of ships exist.
- **Geography:** Earth's field is ~2× stronger near the poles than the equator.
  - Ships fly best in the north (US, UK, USSR, Germany).
  - Struggle near the equator (Africa, Latin America).
  - **South Atlantic Anomaly** (weak field over South America/South Atlantic) is practically a **no-fly zone**.
- **Solar storms** disturb the field. Ships can lose lift and crash. (**[REAL]** Carrington Event, 1859.)
- **Mildly radioactive.** Crews suffer long-term health effects. Crashes contaminate the area: new small dead zone + valuable wreck.

**Timeline**
- 1934: discovered, seized with Fermi's work.
- 1943: hidden as Italy's program vanishes.
- 1945–46: recovered by the major powers.
- 1946–48: desperate development during the hot war; maybe a prototype near the end.
- Early 1950s: first working ships.
- By 1955: only a handful, held by the strongest factions.

**[REAL]** US nuclear aircraft program (1946–61); a B-36 flew with a working reactor (1955–57). Lockheed's CL-1201 (~1970) was a design for a nuclear-powered flying carrier. USS Nautilus, the first nuclear sub, launched 1954. The Soviet Lun ekranoplan (1987) was a missile-armed sea-skimmer. The real nuclear aircraft program failed largely over crew shielding and crash contamination, objections that matter less in a poisoned world.

## Divergence Chain

1. **1934:** Fermi correctly recognizes fission in Rome.
2. Mussolini's regime **raids his lab** and seizes the work as a state secret.
3. **Noddack publishes** her paper. **Hahn & Strassmann** (maybe with **Meitner**) confirm fission.
4. Germany starts a **secret nuclear program**.
5. Italy runs a **weaker program**. Others leave (1938 racial laws). **Fermi's escape: see below.**
6. Bothe makes the graphite mistake. **Peter Jensen catches it.** Germany's program speeds up.
7. Refugee scientists warn the US. **Manhattan Project starts early.** The head start goes into production capacity (more bombs), not an earlier first bomb.
8. US/UK go silent on fission. **Soviets notice** and start their own program.

**[REAL] background:** Fermi caused fission in 1934 but misread it. Noddack's 1934 paper was ignored. Fission was confirmed Dec 1938. Meitner fled Berlin in 1938. Fermi left Italy in Dec 1938 by going to Stockholm for his Nobel and not coming back (wife Laura was Jewish). Bothe & Jensen's graphite error pushed Germany to scarce heavy water. Flyorov's 1942 "silence" letter to Stalin started the Soviet program.

### Fermi's escape (proposed — confirm)
- Problem: if his fission work is a seized state secret, the Nobel can't cite it, and the regime wouldn't let him leave easily.
- **Proposal:** his **slow-neutron discovery** (Oct 1934) seemed harmless, so the regime let him publish it for national prestige. The Nobel cites only that. **Irony:** slow neutrons are key to reactors.
- The regime lets him travel to Stockholm for the prestige, but under watch by the **OVRA** (Fascist secret police). With the racial laws threatening his wife, he defects with his family. A tense escape scene.
- Afterward the regime clamps down on the remaining program, one reason it stays weak.
- **[REAL]** The real Nobel cited "new radioactive elements" and slow-neutron reactions. Fermi's family left Rome by train in Dec 1938 and reached New York in Jan 1939. Niels Bohr reportedly tipped Fermi off about the prize in advance.

### WW2 starts on schedule
- Poland invaded Sept 1939, Pearl Harbor Dec 1941. The war's causes (Hitler's expansion, Versailles, Depression, oil embargo on Japan) are unchanged, and no one has a bomb by 1939.
- Changes are hidden: earlier secret research, uranium buying, scientist recruitment, Soviet nuclear spying from the late 1930s.
- Real conquests quietly become part of the nuclear race: **Czechoslovakia** (Jáchymov uranium, 1939), **Norway** (Vemork heavy water, 1940), **Belgium** (Congo uranium, 1940).
- Players recognize history until D-Day fails, where it visibly breaks.
- **[REAL]** "Deutsche Physik" attacked theoretical physics as "Jewish science"; an SS paper attacked Heisenberg in 1937. In 1940, over 1,000 tons of Congo uranium ore were shipped to New York to keep it from Germany.

### Italy's path
- War follows real history: enters June 1940, Sicily invaded July 1943, Mussolini overthrown, Italy surrenders Sept 1943, Germany occupies the north, Rome liberated June 1944.
- With D-Day failed, Italy may become the main Western front.
- **Mussolini's rescue fails** (Gran Sasso raid). He's handed to the Allies as a prisoner.
  - No puppet state. Germany rules northern Italy directly (or picks someone like Farinacci or Graziani).
  - Bloodier civil war, stronger partisans. Germany seizes more program materials in the north.
  - **Mussolini knows about the program** (his regime seized Fermi's work). He bargains with it or it's extracted in interrogation. Allies learn about Italy's program and get confirmation of Germany's lead.
  - Possible later war crimes trial.
- **The program goes missing in the Sept 1943 chaos**, including the **Hesperium** records. Records, materials and scientists scatter: north with the Germans, south with the king's government, hidden by scientists, or taken by partisans. By 1945 nobody knows where it all went.
- **Who hunts it:** US (Alsos), Soviets, later the dirty bomb factions.
- **Possible Soviet link:** Bruno Pontecorvo.
- **[REAL]** The raid was a near-thing (gliders, overloaded plane). The 1943 surrender terms required handing over Mussolini. Germany moved the Bank of Italy's gold north in 1943. Fermi's team scattered (Segrè, Rasetti abroad; Pontecorvo to Paris 1936, defected to the USSR 1950; Amaldi stayed in Rome and avoided military work). Alsos began its search in Italy.

## The War (1944–45)

**D-Day fails (June 1944)**
- Walkers struggle in the sand and surf and are hard to ship: a major cause of the failure.
- Allies need ~a year to rebuild, mainly landing craft.
- Germany moves troops east. Soviets still win, but **slower and at higher cost**.
- Stalin's suspicion grows: he believes the West let the USSR bleed on purpose.

**Fear of a red Europe**
- Churchill and Roosevelt fear the Soviets will take all of Germany and beyond.
- **Roosevelt dies (real: April 12, 1945). Truman takes over**, inherits the bomb and plan within weeks, and gets pulled along by Churchill.

**US thinks it's first to the bomb**
- It badly underestimates Germany and the Soviets.
- **[REAL]** Groves thought the Soviets were many years away. Stalin already knew about the bomb from spies.

**Germany's V-2 escalation**
- Hitler, desperate, arms V-2s with **crude radioactive payloads and limited chem/bio weapons**.
- Inaccurate, so it hits cities and ports (London, Antwerp). Mostly panic, evacuations, and contamination, not mass destruction.
- Breaks the taboo on unconventional weapons.
- ⚠️ **The large gas stockpiles are NOT used here. Saved for later.**

**Japan's escalation**
- Germany ships materials and know-how to Japan by submarine.
- Japan's nuclear program **fails**, so it turns to **crude bioweapons** (Unit 731), delivered by balloon bombs to the US West Coast, including a **plague attack on San Diego** (based on the real "Cherry Blossoms at Night" plan, which called for submarine-launched planes).
- Somewhat successful: kills people and **terrorizes the US homeland**. America's "London moment."
- Kept limited. ⚠️ **Japan's chemical stockpiles also saved for later.**

**Why the US nukes Germany** (stacked reasons)
- Avoids a second costly D-Day.
- Intelligence says Germany is close to its own bomb.
- Retaliation for the V-2 attacks.
- Ends the war before the Red Army takes Germany. Shows Stalin US power.

**Why the US nukes Japan**
- Retaliation for the bio attacks; huge public pressure.
- A land invasion would be too costly with the US already stretched by the failed D-Day.

**Britain's role**
- V-2 radioactive and bio attacks hit Britain hard. Britain is furious.
- Britain **insists on dropping one bomb on Berlin itself**, flown by the **RAF**.
- Churchill **secretly hopes it scares off the Soviet advance**.
- **[REAL]** Quebec Agreement (1943): neither the US nor UK could use the bomb without the other's consent. Britain's Tube Alloys program (1941) was merged into the Manhattan Project. The Lancaster was considered for carrying the bomb.

**The strikes: 4 US-built bombs, about the same time — late July 1945**
- Goal: hit Germany and Japan before either can retaliate after seeing the first strike.
- **Timing:** days after a Potsdam-style summit where Truman, Churchill and Stalin meet. Truman only vaguely mentions a "new weapon," never the targets. Stalin feels lied to face-to-face.
- Fits: Truman in power, before the planned second D-Day (summer 1945), after the V-2 and balloon campaigns, Soviets on Berlin's outskirts, clear summer weather.
- **[REAL]** Potsdam Conference, July 17–Aug 2, 1945; Truman told Stalin about a new weapon there. Hiroshima's date (Aug 6) depended on clear weather.

| Target | Flown by | Result |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Mittelwerk (V-2 factory, Nordhausen) | US | ❌ **Fails.** Bomber shot down by German air defense. |
| Berlin | RAF | ✅ Succeeds. **Soviet soldiers near Berlin are killed.** |
| Hiroshima | US | ✅ Succeeds. |
| Kyoto | US | ✅ Succeeds. Revenge for San Diego; Stimson's objection overruled. |

- Irony: the US's own German strike fails, while Britain's succeeds.

**Soviet reaction**
- The Soviets **recover the downed bomb near Nordhausen** and identify it as **US-made**.
- Combined with Soviet soldiers killed in Berlin, **Stalin is furious**.
- Stalin believes **Truman has sided with Churchill** against the USSR, and that the strikes were partly aimed at the Red Army.
- The recovered bomb helps the Soviet program reach a bomb by 1946.
- **[REAL]** In March 1945, secret surrender talks between US intelligence and an SS general in Switzerland (Operation Sunrise) made Stalin accuse Roosevelt of betrayal.

**After the strikes, in Europe**
- **Germany never surrenders.** With Berlin gone, it breaks apart. No buffer left between West and Soviets.
- **Mittelwerk survives** (the nuke failed). V-2s and the factory are recovered, and **missile tech advances fast**.
- **Italy's nuclear program fades and goes missing.** (Mystery: who has it?)
- **[REAL]** Nordhausen ended up in the Soviet zone; the Soviets took over the V-2 operation there.

**After the strikes, in Asia**
- Soviets invade Manchuria, Korea, maybe Hokkaido. Fear of a red Asia joins fear of a red Europe.
- Both the US and Soviets capture Unit 731 scientists and research. Sets up bio warfare for 1946.

**Fate of key leaders**
- **Hitler:** dies in the Berlin strike. Body never found, so "Hitler survived" rumors linger.
- **Churchill:** stays in power. (Real 1945 election was only called after Germany surrendered; here Germany never does.)
- **Truman:** survives, but leads an **unpopular, troubled regime**.
- **Stalin:** survives.
- **Mussolini:** Allied prisoner.
- **Hirohito:** **disappears** amid a power struggle between military hawks led by War Minister **Anami Korechika (阿南惟幾)** and the government's doves. *(Details later.)*
- **[REAL]** Stalin told Truman at Potsdam that Hitler might have escaped. Churchill lost the July 1945 election mid-Potsdam and was replaced by Attlee. Anami opposed surrender in Aug 1945; army officers attempted a coup to stop it (Kyūjō incident); Anami killed himself on Aug 15. Leading doves were PM Suzuki, Foreign Minister Tōgō and Navy Minister Yonai.

**Postwar mood (1945–46)**
- **The US knows the Soviets have a working bomb.**
- The US fears it **"overused" the bomb**, and blames **Churchill's Berlin strike** most:
  - Made postwar Germany impossible to clean up.
  - Gave the USSR a reason to use the bomb on them.
- **Truman distrusts Churchill** because of this.
- The US homeland is **scarred by chem and bio attacks**. The public wants the war over and turns **isolationist**.
- But the US is **stuck occupying a fractured Germany** and can't easily leave.
- **Mutual misreading:** the US is war-weary and hesitant; Stalin thinks the US is trigger-happy. Each misjudges the other.
- **[REAL]** Huge "bring the boys home" pressure in 1945–46; US soldiers abroad even protested slow demobilization in Jan 1946. Truman's approval collapsed in 1946, and Republicans took Congress that November. When Churchill gave his "Iron Curtain" speech in March 1946, with Truman on stage, it drew US criticism and Truman publicly distanced himself. The US cut Britain off from nuclear secrets in Aug 1946 (McMahon Act). The US rejected the League of Nations after WW1.

**Britain's own bomb (1946–)**
- The US **cuts Britain off** from nuclear programs and joint efforts.
- Churchill sees a Soviet–West nuclear war as **inevitable**.
- Britain builds its own program **with Canada**, using **its own work plus captured German and Italian technology**.
- Thinking: **nuclear deterrence**, a "hot" version of the Iron Curtain.
- **The Soviets know** (spies inside the program).
- Story links:
  - German scientists held in Britain can be recruited. Italian program pieces may come through Mussolini's interrogation.
  - Britain is within reach of Soviet bombers, so key work may move to **Canada**. Canada could emerge as a nuclear power in the fractured world.
  - Soviet strikes on London in the war partly aim to **kill the British program before it finishes**.
- **[REAL]** Britain and Canada ran a joint lab in Montreal from 1942; Canada's Chalk River reactor (Sept 1945) was the first outside the US. Canada had its own uranium mine. Ten German scientists, including Heisenberg and Hahn, were secretly held and bugged at Farm Hall, England, in 1945. Igor Gouzenko defected in Ottawa in Sept 1945, exposing a Soviet spy ring in Canada that included British physicist Alan Nunn May. Klaus Fuchs joined Britain's program in 1946 and spied for the Soviets until 1950. Britain really decided to build its own bomb in Jan 1947 and tested it in 1952. Bernard Brodie's 1946 book *The Absolute Weapon* laid out early deterrence thinking.

**[REAL] background:** The 1944 Hyde Park agreement only named Japan as a target. Planners worried a dud dropped on Germany could be studied. Groves later wrote that Roosevelt asked about using it on Germany. The US prepared Geiger counters for D-Day in case of a German dirty bomb (Operation Peppermint). Japan had two small, failed nuclear programs. German submarine U-234 tried to ship uranium to Japan in 1945. Japan launched ~9,000 balloon bombs at the US (1944–45); one killed six people in Oregon. Unit 731 planned a plague attack on San Diego. After the war, the US gave Unit 731 leaders immunity for their research. Stalin wanted to land on Hokkaido.

## Escalation to All-Out War (1945–48) — Draft

1. **Scramble for Germany (1945):** West and Soviets race for territory, scientists, weapons. German factions play both sides.
2. **The spark (1946):** Tension builds through unresolved crises in **Iran** (Soviets won't leave, oil) and **Trieste** (Yugoslav shootdowns of US planes). The **first shot is in Germany**: with no surrender, there are no agreed zones. Western and Soviet troops clash over the **Mittelwerk/Nordhausen area** (downed bomb, V-2s, German scientists), and it becomes open war.
   - **Stalin's mindset:** he believes the US already used nukes once to stop the Red Army reaching Berlin, killing his soldiers. So he assumes the **US won't hesitate to nuke rivals again**, and acts on that from the first shot.
   - **[REAL]** Soviet troops stayed in northern Iran into 1946. Yugoslav fighters shot down two US transports over Slovenia in Aug 1946. The US captured Nordhausen in April 1945, removed V-2 parts, then handed it to the Soviets in July 1945 under the zone agreement.
3. **Conventional war:** Soviets push west with far more troops. A war-weary US is slow to respond. **Britain panics** and rushes its nuclear program as a deterrent.
4. **Nuclear exchange.** **Soviet bombers** carry nukes to London, Paris and Western bases. **V-2s carry dirty and chemical payloads**, like Germany's did. The West nukes Soviet supply lines in Poland and Hungary.
   - Soviet bombers: copies of the three US B-29s interned in 1944, built faster under wartime pressure.
   - **[REAL]** Early bombs weighed ~4.5–5 t (Fat Man ~4,670 kg); a V-2 carried ~1 t, with ~320 km range. The first Soviet nuclear missile came in 1956. Three US B-29s landed in the USSR in 1944; the Soviets copied them as the Tu-4 (first flight 1947).
   - **Who fired the first nuke is disputed.** Competing in-world theories:
     - **The Soviets**, bombing London first (Stalin assumed the US would strike; wanted to kill the British program).
     - **Britain**, using a secret nuke on Poland.
     - **Nazi remnants**, using a hidden German nuke to trigger a war between West and East.
     - **The US**, under a war-weary Truman with a deadline to end the situation.
   - **[REAL]** Staged or disputed "first shots": the Gleiwitz incident (1939, Nazi false flag to justify invading Poland), the Mainila shelling (1939, Soviets staged it to justify invading Finland). Hitler and Goebbels hoped until the end that the Allies would turn on each other.
5. **Gas stockpiles released** by both sides.
6. **Plague** (Unit 731 research). Reaches the US via saboteurs, balloons, submarines. Spreads to neutral nations.
7. **Collapse:** capitals destroyed; plague, famine and broken trade finish the rest.

### The hot war: everything, everywhere (mid-1946 → ~1948)
1. **Opening exchange (weeks):** bombers carry nukes to capitals, ports, rail hubs. US bombers hit Soviet cities from Britain, the Middle East and the Pacific. V-2s hit targets within range with dirty payloads.
2. **Everything released (months):** the **saved gas stockpiles finally used** (by bomber, artillery and V-2), plague, dirty bombs from smaller nations and factions. Colonies revolt.
3. **Burnout (1947–48):** bombs, fuel and factories run out. Mutinies, desertion, plague in the armies.

**How the fighting stops:** no victory, no peace treaty. It just stops because no one can keep fighting. Governments lose control, armies become warlord states, and the world slides into the fracture phases. Scattered local fighting continues after ~1948.

**Reach:** Europe, Middle East, Asia hit directly. The US hit by plague, sabotage, submarines, Alaska (not missiles). Latin America, Africa, Australia hit mostly indirectly (plague, famine, broken trade, revolts, local dirty bombs).

**[REAL]** US war plans against the USSR (e.g., Plan Pincher, 1946) planned atomic bombing from Britain, Egypt and Okinawa; a late-1945 report listed ~20 Soviet target cities. WW1 ended largely through collapse (Russia's army in 1917, Germany's Kiel mutiny in 1918). The 1918 flu spread with troop movements. The USSR and Japan never signed a WW2 peace treaty; the Korean War ended with only a ceasefire.

### Fronts of the hot war
| Front | Where | Why |
|-------|-------|-----|
| Central Europe (main) | Germany → Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary | The spark; no buffer; scientists, V-2s, the downed bomb |
| Balkans & Straits | Trieste, Yugoslavia, Greece, Bosporus | Southern gap; only Black Sea–Mediterranean sea route |
| Middle East | Iran, Iraq, Persian Gulf | Oil |
| Asia & Pacific | Manchuria, Korea, China, Japan, Alaska | Unit 731 research, Japan's collapse, China's civil war; Alaska is closest to the USSR |
| Atlantic & US coast | North Atlantic, US shores | Submarines; saboteurs with bio weapons reach the US |
| Scandinavia & Baltic | Danish straits, Norway, Finland | Northern gap; Sweden tries to stay neutral |

**How the front ends up at the Black Curtain:** Soviets push into Germany → the West nukes Soviet supply lines in Poland/Hungary → Soviet armies in Germany are **cut off and stranded** (become German fragment states) → the Soviet front falls back to the Vistula, Carpathians and Danube → contaminated land stalls the West → both sides keep poisoning the line.

**[REAL]** Greek Civil War (1946–49). Soviets demanded joint control of the Turkish Straits in 1946. Britain ran the huge Abadan refinery in Iran. Korea divided at the 38th parallel (Aug 1945). US flew Lend-Lease planes to the USSR via Alaska. German saboteurs were landed by submarine on Long Island and Florida in 1942. Czechoslovak Legion: a stranded army that seized the Trans-Siberian Railway.

- **V-2s reach only ~320 km** (front-line areas and nearby cities) and carry ~1 t: dirty and chemical payloads, not nukes. **Nukes travel by bomber.** Nothing reaches the US by missile.
- **[REAL]** German armies surrendered region by region in 1945. US took von Braun (Operation Paperclip); Soviets deported thousands of German specialists in 1946. Soviets captured the Dyhernfurth nerve agent factory. Trieste (1945) and Iran (1946) were real flashpoints. Germany's A9/A10 "America Rocket" was paper-only.

## The Black Curtain

- **Name:** a play on Churchill's "Iron Curtain." **[REAL]** His March 1946 speech described it running from Stettin (Baltic) to Trieste (Adriatic), the same two western gateway cities.
- **Area:** a slanted box. West edge Gniew → Zagreb, east edge parallel through Lviv. ~900 km long, ~450–500 km wide.
- **Western edge bulges over Silesia** to take in the Dyhernfurth nerve agent site (near Wrocław), where the gas release spread.
- Covers most of Poland, eastern Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, western Ukraine, northern Croatia (Warsaw, Kraków, Silesia, Budapest; maybe Vienna on the edge).
- **Why here:** the front line freezes here between West and East.
- **What poisons it:** nukes on rail hubs, deliberate radioactive barriers, the Dyhernfurth gas stockpile released, toxic "black rain," plague.
- **Lasts to ~1955+:** basic bomb radiation fades fast, but hotspots, lingering chemicals, unexploded munitions and disease keep it unusable. Nobody knows what's safe, so nobody returns.

| Layer | State by ~1955 |
|-------|----------------|
| Core | Deadly. Hotspots, toxic sites, spores. |
| Middle | Dangerous. Survivable briefly with protection. |
| Edges | Slowly recovering. Scavengers and smugglers. |

- Splits the world into a Western side and an Eastern side.
- Flavor: wildlife returns, so the zone is overgrown rather than barren.
- **[REAL]** WWI Zone Rouge (France), still partly closed. Chernobyl Exclusion Zone (1986–today). Gruinard Island, quarantined ~50 years after 1942 anthrax tests. Black rain fell after Hiroshima. MacArthur later said he'd have laid a radioactive belt on the Korea–China border.

### Corridors around the Black Curtain

Old routes through the zone (Berlin–Warsaw–Moscow rail, upper Danube via Bratislava/Budapest) are cut.

**North (Baltic gap)**
- **Land:** narrow coastal strip, Stettin → Gdańsk/Elbląg (dangerous, on the edge) → Königsberg → Kaunas/Vilnius/Riga.
- **Sea:** Kiel, Lübeck, Copenhagen, Stockholm ↔ Riga, Tallinn, Leningrad. Chokepoint: **Danish straits**.
- **Far north backdoor:** Haparanda–Tornio (only Sweden–Finland land link).

**South (Balkan gap)**
- **Land:** Trieste/Ljubljana → past Zagreb → Sava valley → **Belgrade** (major junction) → Niš → Sofia → Istanbul.
- **River:** lower Danube, Belgrade → Iron Gates → Black Sea.
- **Sea:** Adriatic → Mediterranean → Istanbul → Black Sea (Odessa). Chokepoint: **Bosporus/Dardanelles** (Turkey).

**Key cities**
- West frontier: Stettin, Prague, Vienna, Trieste.
- East frontier: Königsberg, Vilnius, Grodno region, Belgrade, Bucharest.
- Neutral gatekeepers: Sweden, Denmark, Turkey.

**[REAL]** German refugees crossed the frozen Vistula Lagoon/Spit in 1945. Haparanda–Tornio was a WWI prisoner and mail exchange point. The Orient Express ran through Zagreb, Belgrade, Sofia to Istanbul. Turkey stayed neutral most of WW2.

## Dirty Bombs (Free for All)

- Once the taboo is gone, **smaller nations and factions build crude dirty bombs** using German/Italian program records.
- **Goal isn't mass deaths.** It's making areas **permanently useless without specialized equipment.**
- **Who:** German/Italian splinter states, neutral/minor powers (Sweden, Switzerland, Spain, Yugoslavia, Argentina), civil war factions (e.g., China), whoever has Italy's missing program.
- **Used on:** enemy capitals, ports, water, farmland.
- **Deterrence:** tiny states can threaten big neighbors, so no one can safely conquer anyone. Nations stay broken up.
- **Downside:** crude programs sicken their own workers and contaminate their own towns.
- Result: **smaller dead zones scattered worldwide**, beyond the Black Curtain.
- **[REAL]** Oppenheimer and Fermi discussed radioactive poisoning of German food (1943). US Army researched radiological weapons into the early 1950s. Sweden (1945) and Switzerland (1946) started secret nuclear studies. Argentina's Huemul Project was a fraud.

## How the World Fractures (1947–55)

The war destroys what holds nations together (capitals, transport, food, trust). Old fault lines do the rest.

1. **Collapse (1947–48):** Governments exist on paper but can't reach their territory. Plague spreads. Armies are cut off abroad. Whoever feeds and protects people becomes the real authority.
2. **Breakup (1948–50):** Generals, stranded armies, regional governors, ethnic movements, and whoever controls food, water, factories or weapons take over.
3. **Frozen world (1950–55):** Borders form around safe land, water, food, fuel, factories and equipment. **Dirty bomb deterrence freezes them.** The Black Curtain splits East from West.

**Faction types:** military remnants, old governments, ideological splinters, ethnic/regional states, scientific groups (nuclear/bio knowledge, maybe Italy's program), scavengers/smugglers/raiders.

**[REAL]** Europe nearly starved in winter 1946–47; the Marshall Plan saved it (no Marshall Plan here). The Czechoslovak Legion (~40,000 stranded soldiers) seized the Trans-Siberian Railway in the Russian Civil War. China's Warlord Era (1916–28). Ukrainian and Baltic guerrillas fought the Soviets into the 1950s.

### Latin America
- Most of it was **already independent** (since the 1810s–20s). Colonies left were in the Caribbean and Guianas, plus Puerto Rico.
- "Independence" here means breaking from US influence, colonies breaking away, and regions splitting from countries.
- Devolves into a mix of:
  - **US companies operating as local military forces** (guarding plantations, mines, oil).
  - **Regional and independence leaders** with different agendas and ideologies.
  - **Governments trying to keep control.**
- Possible hooks: the **Panama Canal Zone** (stranded US garrison at a key chokepoint); Nazi refugees and "Hitler escaped to Argentina" rumors.
- **[REAL]** United Fruit dominated Central America; in 1928 Colombian troops massacred striking United Fruit workers (Banana Massacre). Ford built a company town in the Amazon (Fordlândia). Big US firms ran Chile's copper mines. São Paulo revolted against Brazil in 1932; Mexico's Cristero War (1926–29). Perón elected in Argentina in 1946. ### Africa
